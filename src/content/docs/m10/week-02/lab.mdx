## Goal

**Work out how much silicon one {chip.name} uses, how many fit on a <GlossaryTerm term="wafer" />, and what one chip costs.**

Why it matters for chips: before a chip is made, engineers estimate its cost exactly like this. A chip that is 10% smaller fits more copies on each wafer, so every chip gets cheaper. Scripts like yours let them try many sizes in seconds.

## What you'll practise

- [Lesson A: expr, Tcl as a calculator](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-02/lesson-a/): operators, whole numbers and decimals, the integer-division trap.
- [Lesson B: Square brackets](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-02/lesson-b/): storing results with `set` and `[ ]`.

## Specification

The factory makes {chip.name} on a square test wafer, 10000 µm (10 mm) on each side. Real wafers are round; a square keeps the maths simple. The whole wafer costs 1980 dollars. The chips sit in a grid: rows of chips side by side, and rows stacked from top to bottom.

- **Inputs.** The starter gives you four lines: `set chipWidth 400`, `set chipHeight 300`, `set waferSide 10000` and `set waferCost 1980`. **Keep these four lines exactly as they are.** The checker changes their values and runs your script again, to test that you computed every number instead of typing it.
- **Output.** With these inputs, your script must print exactly:

<pre class="tc-code" tabindex="0">{expected.trimEnd()}</pre>

- **Only whole chips count.** A chip that hangs over the edge of the wafer is thrown away.
- Words, capitals and units must match. Extra spaces are forgiven. A decimal counts as right if it differs only in its very last digits.

## Step by step

Press **Run** after every step, and compare your new line with the specification.

1. **Area in µm².** Store the chip's area in a variable called `chipArea`, then print the first line. `um2` means µm².
2. **Area in mm².** One mm² is 1000000 µm². Print the second line. You need a decimal here.
3. **Chips per row.** How many chips fit side by side across the wafer, each `chipWidth` wide? Store the answer in `perRow` and print it.
4. **Rows.** How many rows fit from the top of the wafer to the bottom, each `chipHeight` high? Store it in `rows` and print it.
5. **Chips per wafer.** Use `perRow` and `rows`. Store the answer in `perWafer` and print it.
6. **Cost per chip.** Divide the wafer's cost by the number of chips on it. Watch out for the trap from Lesson A.

## Check your work

- **Browser:** press **Check my work** in the Playground tab.
- **Terminal:** in the lab folder, run `tclsh tests/test.tcl`.

You get one line per test:

- `PASS`: this part is right.
- `FAIL`: you see what was expected, what your script printed, often a hint, and which lesson section to revisit.
- `TODO`: you have not done this step yet.
- `NOTE`: the checker could not run a test. It tells you which input line to put back.

The last line is `RESULT: PASS 9/9` when everything is right. Fix one `FAIL` at a time, from the top: a wrong number early on makes every later number wrong too.
