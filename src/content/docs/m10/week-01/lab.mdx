## Goal

**Write a <GlossaryTerm term="script" /> that prints the passport of {chip.name}: its name, size, blocks and layers.**

Why it matters for chips: every chip-design tool starts a job by printing a summary like this one, so that engineers can check at a glance that they are working on the right chip. Later in the course, your scripts will work these facts out from the layout itself. Today you print them.

## What you'll practise

- [Lesson A: Your first script](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-01/lesson-a/): `puts`, double quotes, comments.
- [Lesson B: Commands are words](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-01/lesson-b/): `set` and `$`.

## Specification

Your script must print exactly these five lines, in this order:

<pre class="tc-code" tabindex="0">{passport.trimEnd()}</pre>

- **Exact text.** Capitals, spelling and punctuation must match. Extra spaces are forgiven.
- **Use the variables.** The starter gives you three lines: `set chipName`, `set chipWidth` and `set chipHeight`. **Keep these three lines exactly as they are.** The checker changes their values and runs your script again, to test that your Name and Size lines use `$chipName`, `$chipWidth` and `$chipHeight`.
- **Sign it.** Put your name in the `# Made by:` <GlossaryTerm term="comment" />.

## Step by step

Press **Run** after every step. Seeing your output grow one line at a time is the fastest way to spot a mistake.

1. **Title.** Under `# Step 1`, print `CHIP PASSPORT`.
2. **Name.** Print the Name line. Write `$chipName` where the name goes, not the name itself.
3. **Size.** Print the Size line, with `$chipWidth` and `$chipHeight` in it. The letters `um` stand for µm.
4. **Blocks and layers.** Print the last two lines. You may type these names: lists of names come in Week 5.
5. **Test your variables.** Change `400` to `500` in `set chipWidth 400` and press Run. Does your Size line say 500 now? Then change it back to 400.
6. **Sign it.** Replace `???` in `# Made by: ???` with your name.

## Check your work

- **Browser:** press **Check my work** in the Playground tab.
- **Terminal:** in the lab folder, run `tclsh tests/test.tcl`.

You get one line per test:

- `PASS`: this part is right.
- `FAIL`: you see what was expected, what your script printed, and which lesson section to revisit.
- `TODO`: you have not done this step yet.
- `NOTE`: the checker could not run a test. It tells you what to put back, usually one of the three `set` lines.

The last line is `RESULT: PASS 9/9` when everything is right. A result like `RESULT: FAIL 6/9` means 6 tests pass so far. Fix one `FAIL` at a time, starting from the top: one early mistake often causes the later ones.
