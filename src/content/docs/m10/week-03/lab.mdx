## Goal

**Predict the exact output of 15 short scripts about {chip.name}, then let the checker tell you which predictions were right.**

**This lab trains prediction. Predict first, run to check.** Running a puzzle before you predict skips the part that teaches you. A wrong prediction is the most useful thing you can get here, because it shows you exactly which rule to fix.

Why it matters for chips: layout scripts build names, coordinates and commands out of text. One stray `$` or a missing brace puts a wrong shape on the wrong layer, and you may not notice until much later. People who can read a line the way Tcl reads it find those bugs in seconds.

## What you'll practise

- [Lesson A: Quotes vs braces](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-03/lesson-a/): grouping, the outer grouping decides, a new command inside `[ ]`.
- [Lesson B: Backslashes, the rules, and braced expr](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-03/lesson-b/): `\` escapes, one-pass <GlossaryTerm term="substitution" />, comments, braced `expr`.

## Specification

- **Input:** 15 puzzles in three rounds (below). Each puzzle is a short script that prints something.
- **Output:** in the starter file, one line per puzzle, `p01` to `p15`. Replace `???` with exactly what you think the puzzle prints.
- **Write each prediction inside braces.** Braces keep your text exactly as typed, so `$`, `[ ]`, `\` and `"` in your prediction stay as they are. Every puzzle is designed so that its output can be written this way.
- **Two lines of output?** Put both lines inside the braces, on two lines. Spaces at the start or end of a line, and empty lines before or after your text, are ignored, so you may indent.

**Example.** This script is not one of the 15:

```tcl run expect="Block: PADS"
set block PADS
puts "Block: $block"
```

For it you would write `set p00 {Block: PADS}`, because the quotes allow `$block` to become `PADS`.

## Check your work

- **Browser:** press **Check my work** in the Playground tab.
- **Terminal:** in the lab folder, run `tclsh tests/test.tcl`.

You get one line per puzzle:

- `PASS`: your prediction is right.
- `FAIL`: you see what Tcl prints, what you wrote, and which lesson section to revisit.
- `TODO`: you have not answered that puzzle yet.

The last line is `RESULT: PASS 15/15` when every prediction is right. A result like `RESULT: FAIL 12/15` means 12 are right so far.

For each `FAIL`, write one sentence that names the rule you missed before you change your answer. Fixing the idea matters more than fixing the line.
