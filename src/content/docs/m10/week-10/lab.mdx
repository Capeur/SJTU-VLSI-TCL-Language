Week 10 has no separate lab. Its lab time goes to the M10 <GlossaryTerm term="capstone" />, then to the exam. Both use everything from Weeks 1 to 10.

## 1. The capstone: Mini Floorplan Checker (2 to 3 hours)

**[Open the capstone](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/capstone/).** You check a floorplan of {chip.name} with four proposed new blocks and three badly typed entries, and print a report. It reuses `rectArea` and `rectOverlaps` from Week 7, `format` from Week 8, and `try` from this week.

A plan that works:

1. **Session 1 (about 1 hour): the procs.** Write `rectArea`, `rectOverlaps`, `blockRect`, `rectInside` and `checkBlock`. Press **Check my work** after each one: the checker tests every proc on its own, so you see your progress before the report even exists.
2. **Session 2 (about 1 hour): the report.** Print the lines one kind at a time, from the first line down to `Problems`. Check after each kind.
3. **Session 3 (30 minutes or more): polish.** Make the hidden floorplans pass, then score your readability with the list on the capstone page.

You do not have to finish in one sitting. Copy your script somewhere safe between sessions: the playground does not save it.

## 2. The M10 exam (45 minutes)

**[Open the exam](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/exam/)** when the capstone is done, ideally on another day. It has 30 items; two thirds of them ask "what does this print?", and a third are about Week 3, quotes and substitution. Answer without your notes and without the playground.

## How they are checked

- **The capstone** is checked like the weekly labs, by **Check my work** or `tclsh tests/test.tcl`, with points: correctness 50, error handling 25. Readability, the last 25 points, you score yourself.
- **The exam** is checked by running each item's code in your browser. The page stores no answers, and nothing is sent anywhere.
