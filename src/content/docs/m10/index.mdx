M10 takes you from your first `puts` to a 100-line script that stores, checks and reports data about the rectangles of a chip. Everything runs in your browser.

## By the end of M10 you will be able to

1. Predict exactly what Tcl does with quotes, braces, brackets, `$` and backslashes.
2. Compute with `expr`, and make decisions and loops with `if`, `while`, `for` and `foreach`.
3. Store and search data in lists, dicts and arrays, and clean and format text.
4. Write your own commands with `proc`, with arguments, defaults and the right scope.
5. Refuse bad input with clear errors, catch them with `try`, and keep going.

## The capstone: Mini Floorplan Checker

In [the M10 capstone](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/capstone/), your script checks a floorplan of {chip.name} with proposed new blocks and badly typed entries. It refuses the bad entries with `try`, computes the areas, finds the blocks that stick out of the chip and every pair of blocks that clash, and prints a formatted report.

It is the first of four <GlossaryTerm term="capstone">capstones</GlossaryTerm> that build on each other. In M20, its rectangles and its procs `rectArea` and `rectOverlaps` become TinyLayout, a small layout database of your own. In Week 23 you will draw these rectangles for real, as polygons in a layout, and open them in KLayout.

## Where the code runs

All of M10 runs in the **browser playground**: nothing to install. Every lesson has playgrounds, and every lab and the capstone have a **Check my work** button. If you like, you can also install `tclsh` early (see [Setup](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/setup/)) and run the labs' `tests/test.tcl` in a terminal. From Week 11 on, `tclsh` on your own computer is required.

## How the lessons work in M10

M10 gives you the most help of the whole course, because everything is new:

- Each lesson shows **two worked examples**, explained line by line, then a **faded example** with blanks to fill.
- **Predict → Run → Explain** is the main exercise: you write down what you think the code prints before you run it.
- Labs go **step by step**, with a checker that tells you what to fix and which lesson to revisit.

From M20 on, you will get one worked example, then a broken script to fix: you will be ready for less help.

## Assessment

- **Weekly quizzes:** 10 questions, about 70 % on the week and 30 % from earlier weeks. Missed questions come back in your spaced review.
- **[M10 exam](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/exam/):** 30 items; at least 60 % are "what does this print?", and a third are on Week 3. Each answer is checked by running Tcl in your browser.
- **Capstone:** correctness 50 %, error handling 25 % (both checked automatically), readability 25 % (you score it with a short list).
