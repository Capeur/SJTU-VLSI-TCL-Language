## Goal

**Rank a small library of standard cells from the largest to the smallest, and report on the ranking.**

Why it matters for chips: the CORE block of {chip.name} is built from thousands of <GlossaryTerm term="standard-cell">standard cells</GlossaryTerm>, small ready-made pieces such as an inverter (INV) or a NAND gate. They all have the same height, so they line up in rows, like houses along a street. Their widths differ. A designer who wants a smaller chip first looks at the cells that take the most room. Your script makes that list.

## Specification

- **Inputs.** The starter gives you three lines. `set cells {…}` is a <GlossaryTerm term="nested-list">nested list</GlossaryTerm>: each cell is a pair `{name {x1 y1 x2 y2}}`, its name and then its rectangle, in µm. `set topCount 3` says how many of the largest cells to name. `set findCell NOR2` is the cell whose rank you look up. **Keep these three lines exactly as they are.** The checker changes their values twice and runs your script again.
- **Output.** With these inputs, your script must print exactly:

<pre class="tc-code" tabindex="0">{expected.trimEnd()}</pre>

- **Line 1** counts the cells.
- **Then one line per cell, largest area first**, numbered from 1. The area is (x2 − x1) × (y2 − y1). Two cells with the same area keep the order they were given in; lsort already does that for you.
- **The Largest line** names the `topCount` largest cells, with `, ` between them.
- **The last line** is the rank of `findCell`: `NOR2: rank 2`. If the cell is not in the list, print `NOR2: not found` (with its name).
- Words, capitals and punctuation must match. Extra spaces are forgiven.

The numbers are teaching numbers. Real standard cells are about a micrometre wide, a hundred times smaller.

## Step by step

Press **Run** after every step, and compare your new lines with the specification.

1. **Count.** Each cell is one element of `cells`, so llength counts the cells.
2. **Pairs.** Start an empty list. For each cell, take its name with `lindex $cell 0` and its rectangle with `lindex $cell 1`, compute the area, and lappend `[list $name $area]`.
3. **Sort and print.** Sort the pairs with `lsort -integer -decreasing -index 1` and store the result. Then print one numbered line per pair, with a counter.
4. **Names.** Make the list of names in sorted order with lmap: the body is `lindex $pair 0`.
5. **Largest.** Take the first `topCount` names with lrange, glue them with `join … ", "`, and print the line.
6. **Rank.** Find `findCell` in the names with lsearch. If the index is not −1, the rank is the index + 1. Otherwise, print that it was not found.
