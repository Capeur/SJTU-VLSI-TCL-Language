## Goal

**Store METAL1 rectangles as lists, work out their sizes, and find the ones that are too narrow for the factory.**

Why it matters for chips: in a layout file, every shape is just numbers: the <GlossaryTerm term="coordinate">coordinates</GlossaryTerm> of its corners. Layout tools read those numbers in long flat lists, four at a time for a rectangle. In Week 23 you will hand lists like these to a layout tool and see them drawn. Today your script reads them and checks them against a <GlossaryTerm term="design-rule">design rule</GlossaryTerm>, like the Week 4 lab, but for many shapes at once.

## What you'll practise

- [Lesson B: foreach and lappend](/SJTU-VLSI-TCL-Language/m10/week-05/lesson-b/): several loop variables, building a list.

## Specification

Inside the CORE block of {chip.name}, some METAL1 rectangles have been drawn. Each one is a <GlossaryTerm term="list" /> of four coordinates in µm, `{x1 y1 x2 y2}`: the bottom-left corner, then the top-right corner.

- **Inputs.** The starter gives you three lines: `set rect {30 40 90 70}`, one rectangle; `set shapes {…}`, three rectangles in one flat list, four numbers each; and `set minWidth 3`, the METAL1 minimum width. **Keep these three lines exactly as they are.** The checker changes their values twice and runs your script again.
- **Output.** With these inputs, your script must print exactly:

<pre class="tc-code" tabindex="0">{expected.trimEnd()}</pre>

- **Lines 1 to 3** are the width, height and area of `rect`. The width is x2 − x1 and the height is y2 − y1.
- **Then one line per shape** in `shapes`, numbered from 1, then the number of shapes and their total area.
- **Too narrow.** A shape is too narrow if its width **or** its height is less than `minWidth`. A side exactly as wide as `minWidth` is fine. Print one line `Too narrow: shape N` for each narrow shape, in order. If no shape is too narrow, print `Too narrow: none`.
- Words, capitals and units must match. Extra spaces are forgiven.

## Step by step

Press **Run** after every step, and compare your new lines with the specification.

1. **Width.** Use `lindex $rect 2` and `lindex $rect 0` inside `expr` to compute the width of `rect`, store it, and print line 1.
2. **Height.** The same with indexes 3 and 1.
3. **Area.** Width times height.
4. **One line per shape.** Set a counter to 0. Write `foreach {x1 y1 x2 y2} $shapes {…}`. In the body, add 1 to the counter, compute the width, height and area, and print the shape line.
5. **Count and total.** Before the loop, set a total to 0. In the body, add each area to it. After the loop, print the two lines.
6. **Too narrow.** Before the loop, set an empty list. In the body, if the shape is too narrow, lappend its number. After the loop, print `Too narrow: none` if the list is empty, otherwise one line per number with a second foreach.
