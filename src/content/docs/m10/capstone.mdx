## Goal

**Write a checker that reads a floorplan, refuses the badly typed entries, and reports every block that sticks out of the chip and every pair of blocks that clash.**

Why it matters for chips: the team wants to add four small blocks to {chip.name}: TIMER, a clock; LDO, a voltage regulator; BIAS, which feeds steady currents to the sensor; and TEST, a strip of test pads. Somebody typed the list, and three entries came out wrong. Before anyone draws a single shape, your checker says which proposals fit. This is the first of four <GlossaryTerm term="capstone">capstones</GlossaryTerm>: in M20, your checker grows into TinyLayout, and in Week 23 you will draw these rectangles for real.

## Specification

### Inputs

The starter gives you four lines. **Keep them exactly as they are:** the checker changes them three times, to hidden floorplans, and runs your script again.

- `chipName`, `chipWidth`, `chipHeight`: the chip is the rectangle from (0, 0) to (`chipWidth`, `chipHeight`), in µm.
- `blocks`: a list of entries `{name x y w h}`: a <GlossaryTerm term="block" />'s name, its bottom-left corner, then its width and height. Some entries are bad.

### The procs

The checker calls these procs with its own test cases, so use exactly these names and arguments.

| Proc | Returns |
|---|---|
| `rectArea rect` | the area of `{x1 y1 x2 y2}` |
| `rectOverlaps a b` | `1` if the two rectangles overlap **or touch**, `0` if there is space between them (Week 7's rule) |
| `blockRect block` | the corners `{x y x+w y+h}` of a block `{name x y w h}` |
| `rectInside rect boundary` | `1` if the rectangle is inside the boundary rectangle, **touching its edge included**; `0` if not |
| `checkBlock block` | nothing for a good block; for a bad one, it raises an error with one of the messages below |

`checkBlock`'s <GlossaryTerm term="guard-clause">guard clauses</GlossaryTerm>, in this order, with exactly these messages:

1. The entry must have 5 values: `needs 5 values, got 4`.
2. `x`, `y`, `w` and `h`, in this order, must be numbers (`string is double -strict`): `w is not a number: ten`.
3. `w`, then `h`, must be positive: `w must be positive, got -5`. Zero is not positive.

### The two rules

- **Blocks must not touch each other.** Touching counts as a conflict, along an edge or at a corner, exactly as in Week 7: two touching blocks leave no room between them for wires.
- **Blocks may touch the edge of the chip.** The boundary is a limit, not a neighbour. A block that reaches the chip's edge has nothing on the other side that needs room: it is still inside the chip. So `rectInside` uses `>=` on the left and bottom, and `<=` on the right and top.

These are design decisions for floorplan blocks, not laws of nature: real layouts often <GlossaryTerm term="abut" /> small cells on purpose.

### The report

With the starter's inputs, your script must print exactly:

<pre class="tc-code" tabindex="0">{expected.trimEnd()}</pre>

Line by line:

- **First line:** the chip's name and size, from the inputs.
- **One `Skipped` line per bad entry**, in input order: `Skipped NAME: ` and the message of `checkBlock`. The name is the first value of the entry.
- **`Skipped: N`**: the number of bad entries. Print it even when N is 0.
- **One area line per good block**, in input order. Blocks outside the chip get an area line too.
- **`Total`**: the sum of the areas of the good blocks that are **inside** the chip only, and their share of the chip's area, rounded to 1 decimal: `Total 83480 um2 (69.6% of the chip)`. TEST is outside, so its 1200 µm² is not counted.
- **One `Outside` line** per good block that is not inside the chip, in input order.
- **One `Conflict` line per clashing pair** of good blocks, each pair once, in the pair loop's order (i from 0, j from i + 1). Blocks outside the chip are checked for conflicts too.
- **`Problems`**: the number of Outside lines plus the number of Conflict lines. Skipped entries are **not** problems: they are reported, but they are not part of the floorplan.

Words, capitals and punctuation must match. Extra spaces are forgiven, so you can line up the columns with `format` as you like.

## Check your work

- **Browser:** press **Check my work** in the Playground tab.
- **Terminal:** in the lab folder, run `tclsh tests/test.tcl`.

You get one line per test, with its points:

- **Correctness, 50 points:** each proc on its own test cases (20), the report for the starter's inputs (10), and three hidden floorplans (20). For a hidden floorplan, the checker shows your line and says what kind of line was expected; it never shows the expected text.
- **Error handling, 25 points:** `checkBlock`'s three kinds of error (10), the Skipped lines and a report that goes on to the end (10), and the use of `try` (5).

The last lines are `RESULT: PASS 17/17` when every automatic test passes, then your score, for example `SCORE: 75/75 automatic`.

## Readability: score yourself (25 points)

A checker cannot judge whether your code is easy to read, so you do. Give yourself 5 points for each line that is true, or ask a classmate to score it.

1. Every proc has a clear name, and does one job.
2. Every `expr`, `if` and `while` condition is in braces.
3. The code is indented by 4 spaces inside every body.
4. No result is typed in by hand: every number in the report comes from the inputs.
5. A comment before each step says what it does.

Your capstone mark: the checker's score out of 75, plus your readability score out of 25.
